And you don't necessarily need a crankshaft

That's probably the most interesting part.

A normal piston engine converts linear motion into rotation through:

piston→connecting rod→crankshaft

Your architecture could instead go:

plasma→linear magnet→electricity

and then:

electricity→traction motor

So mechanically, the engine can become much simpler.

It's similar in overall philosophy to a free-piston linear generator, except your proposed working-fluid and field-control stages are different.

The upper field could also return the piston

You could potentially use the upper coil assembly in two modes.

During the power stroke:

moving magnet→coil→electric generation

Then afterward, the power electronics reverse the stator behavior:

coil current→magnetic force→return piston

So the same coil assembly becomes both:

generator + electromagnetic return spring

That gives you an electronically controlled stroke.

No crankshaft required.

One important change I'd make

I would not put permanent magnets directly in the hottest part of the plasma chamber.

Strong permanent magnets can lose performance when overheated, and some permanently demagnetize above their operating range.

Instead:

PLASMA CHAMBER
     │
 ceramic / thermal barrier
     │
 nonmagnetic piston face
     │
 magnetic translator
     │
 induction coils

So plasma pushes a high-temperature piston face, while the magnet sits farther away in a cooler translator section.

Or even better, use a ferromagnetic/soft-magnetic translator and keep the strongest permanent magnets or electromagnets in the stationary stator.

That lowers the moving mass and improves thermal survival.

Now phase three of these assemblies by roughly 120°. Instead of a crankshaft mechanically synchronizing pistons, electromagnetic control becomes the synchronization mechanism.

There are also several operating modes worth putting into the POC. In generator mode, both plasma/pressure events accelerate the translators and the coils extract electrical energy. In regenerative braking, the coils deliberately oppose translator motion and recover energy. In electromagnetic return mode, stored electrical energy reverses coil current to help return the translators. And in a differential mode, the controller intentionally changes the top/bottom field strengths to alter stroke timing or force.

The particularly elegant version would not have the two magnets collide in the center. Give each translator a bounded stroke and put the central region under active electromagnetic control. The machine then resembles two linear alternators facing each other.
