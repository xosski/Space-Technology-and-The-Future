The key issue is that an electric-only EVAPS vehicle has no primary onboard energy source other than its battery. Plasma is therefore an intermediate working state, not fuel.

The existing VAPS documents already use the right accounting principle: plasma formation and electromagnetic hardware have to be counted as parasitic loads rather than additional energy sources.

What could EVAPS potentially beat?
Against gasoline vehicles

If the electric source is grid-charged batteries, you'd expect:

zero tailpipe CO₂;
no gasoline combustion;
much lower local NOx/HC/CO;
potentially less mechanical vibration;
fewer conventional engine components;
instantaneous electrical torque;
regenerative braking;
potentially sophisticated energy recovery.

Even an inefficient electric-plasma prototype could therefore have some characteristics superior to an ICE.

But ordinary EVs already possess most of those advantages.

Against today's EVs

This is the difficult comparison.

To justify EVAPS, you'd need to demonstrate something an ordinary motor/inverter doesn't already do better.

For example, one of these would be significant:

1. Exceptional power density

If the plasma/PAIR/PCRAS system produced dramatically more power per kilogram than conventional motors, it could be valuable even with somewhat lower efficiency.

2. Very high transient output

KERS + plasma machinery might produce extremely high short-duration power.

That could matter in racing, aerospace or specialized machinery.

3. Thermal advantages

If your plasma architecture could make productive use of thermal conditions that ordinary motors cannot, there could be specialized applications.

4. Novel energy recovery

This is probably the biggest one.

What I would target numerically

For an EVAPS research program, I wouldn't promise a 500-mile vehicle or enormous horsepower yet.

I'd establish gates.

Generation/conditioning POC: demonstrate a repeatable change in plasma conductivity or another useful property versus the control.

Extraction POC: demonstrate that conditioned plasma improves electrical extraction.

PAIR/PCRAS POC: aim for >70% plasma/flow-to-electrical conversion eventually; below ~50%, the architecture becomes increasingly difficult to justify for vehicle use.

The surprising conclusion

Your electric-only EVAPS is probably not the best replacement for an EV motor.

But it could potentially become something more unusual:

an electrically controlled plasma energy-conditioning and recovery subsystem integrated into an otherwise conventional electric drivetrain.

That distinction matters.

The conventional traction motor handles what motors are exceptionally good at.

PAIR/PCRAS handles plasma-flow energy extraction.

KERS handles violent transient energy.

The battery handles long-duration storage.

And the controller decides where energy goes.

The resulting vehicle isn't really a “plasma car.”

It's a multi-domain electric energy-management architecture:

Electrical↔Electromagnetic↔Plasma↔Rotational↔Kinetic
