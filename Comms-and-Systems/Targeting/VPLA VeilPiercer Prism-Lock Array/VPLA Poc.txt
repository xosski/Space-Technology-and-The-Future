VPLA — VeilPiercer Prism-Lock Array
Defensive Proof of Concept for Multi-Angle Optical Ranging and Confidence-Scored Contact Tracking

Document Type: Defensive tracking and navigation white-paper proof of concept
System Type: Multi-beam optical/IR triangulation and trajectory-estimation array
Primary Use: Spacecraft navigation, collision avoidance, docking support, debris tracking, and persistent contact awareness
Status: High-level architecture draft
Doctrine Line: One beam gives a line. Several beams give a location.

1. Executive Summary

The VeilPiercer Prism-Lock Array (VPLA) is a defensive sensing architecture that uses multiple optical or infrared emitters, distributed across a spacecraft or sensor frame, to observe the same contact from different known geometries.

Rather than relying on one optical line of sight, VPLA combines multiple beam directions, known emitter positions, range measurements where available, ship motion, and repeated observations over time to estimate:

$$ \text{position} $$ $$ \text{velocity} $$ $$ \text{trajectory} $$ $$ \text{uncertainty} $$ $$ \text{confidence} $$

The concept extends the existing VeilPiercer Parallax Engine, which already fuses visual bearing, own-ship motion, radar/minimap information, and time continuity to estimate contact coordinates.

It also follows the existing VeilPiercer Relay Mesh principle that several synchronized viewpoints can produce a stronger track than one uncertain observation.

2. Concept Thesis

A single laser or infrared beam primarily provides a line of sight.

Several spatially separated emitters provide multiple lines of sight.

If those emitter locations and orientations are known, their observations can be fused geometrically to estimate the contact's three-dimensional position.

Conceptually:

The system therefore converts:

$$ \text{multiple bearings} $$

into:

$$ \text{one best-fit contact state} $$

rather than treating any single sensor as perfect.

3. Core Hypothesis

If the spacecraft knows:

the position of each emitter,
the pointing direction of each beam,
the exact observation time,
its own orientation and motion,
and optionally the measured optical range,

then multiple observations can be fused into a more precise track than a single observation.

Mathematically, an emitter at:

$$ E_i $$

with measured direction:

$$ \hat d_i $$

defines an observation ray:

$$ L_i(t)=E_i+t\hat d_i $$

For ideal measurements, the rays intersect.

Real measurements contain error, so VPLA instead estimates the point:

$$ P^* = \arg\min_P \sum_i d(P,L_i)^2 $$

where:

$$ d(P,L_i) $$

is the distance between candidate point \(P\) and observation ray \(L_i\).

The resulting solution is therefore a best-fit estimate, not claimed absolute truth.

4. Why Multiple Angles Matter

The primary advantage is baseline geometry.

Consider two sensors separated by:

$$ b $$
A ●-------------------------● B
       known baseline

       \                   /
        \                 /
         \               /
          \             /
             ● CONTACT

If the apparent angular difference is:

$$ \Delta\theta $$

then a simplified range relationship is:

$$ R\approx\frac{b}{\tan(\Delta\theta)} $$

The exact implementation would use full three-dimensional geometry and filtering, but the important concept remains:

Angular separation plus a known baseline constrains distance.

This is complementary to the existing VeilPiercer Parallax Engine, which improves range estimates as the ship itself moves and creates parallax over time.

VPLA creates several such baselines simultaneously.

6. Layered Architecture
L1 — Own-Ship Reference State

The spacecraft supplies the reference frame.

Required data includes:

position,
velocity,
acceleration,
heading,
pitch,
yaw,
roll,
sensor mounting geometry,
timestamp.

The existing Parallax Engine already identifies own-ship telemetry as the anchor required to distinguish contact motion from platform or camera motion.

L2 — Optical/IR Emitter Array

Several emitters are mounted at known locations.

Example:

Port emitter
Starboard emitter
Dorsal emitter
Ventral emitter
Forward emitter

Each unit maintains:

position relative to ship center
orientation
beam direction
wavelength/channel
timestamp
health state

Spatial separation between emitters creates the triangulation baseline.

L3 — Optical Return / Observation Layer

Each channel records available measurements such as:

azimuth,
elevation,
return timing,
optical intensity,
signal quality,
apparent target centroid,
channel confidence.

No one channel is assumed authoritative.

L4 — Contact Correlator

The system determines whether different beam returns correspond to the same observed object.

Correlation factors can include:

observation time,
angular proximity,
range consistency,
motion consistency,
optical signature similarity,
previously established track history.

The existing Parallax Engine uses comparable bearing, timing, persistence, and motion agreement to correlate radar and visual contacts.

L5 — Prism-Lock Geometry Solver

This layer fuses the observations.

Input:

Emitter A position + bearing
Emitter B position + bearing
Emitter C position + bearing

Output:

Estimated X
Estimated Y
Estimated Z
Geometric residual
Uncertainty volume

The more consistently the observation rays converge, the stronger the geometry score becomes.

L6 — Range Resolver

Where round-trip optical timing is available:

$$ R=\frac{c\Delta t}{2} $$

where:

\(R\) = range,
\(c\) = propagation speed,
\(\Delta t\) = measured round-trip travel time.

That range becomes an additional constraint rather than replacing angular triangulation.

This gives:

$$ \text{bearing} + \text{range} + \text{baseline geometry} $$

instead of only one of those variables.

L7 — Trajectory Estimator

Repeated measurements generate a state over time.

A basic track state could be:

$$ X= [x,y,z,v_x,v_y,v_z] $$

where:

\(x,y,z\) = position,
\(v_x,v_y,v_z\) = velocity.

Repeated solutions yield:

T0 → P0
T1 → P1
T2 → P2
T3 → P3

which produces:

position
velocity
direction
trajectory estimate

This mirrors the Relay Mesh's existing concept of maintaining a contact through spatial position, time continuity, and observation quality rather than representing it as a static dot.

8. Weighted Sensor Fusion

Suppose each channel estimates a contact location:

$$ P_A,P_B,P_C $$

with corresponding confidence:

$$ w_A,w_B,w_C $$

A simplified fused estimate is:

$$ P_f= \frac{ w_AP_A+w_BP_B+w_CP_C }{ w_A+w_B+w_C } $$

The actual implementation should also consider covariance and geometry, but the principle is:

A weak observation contributes less than a strong one.

18. Collision-Avoidance Integration

If the predicted paths of ship and contact are:

$$ P_s(t) $$

and:

$$ P_c(t) $$

then the navigation layer can calculate closest approach:

$$ D_{min} = \min_t \left| P_s(t)-P_c(t) \right| $$

If:

$$ D_{min}<D_{safe} $$

the operator receives a deconfliction warning.

That aligns directly with the defensive awareness and collision-avoidance purpose already established in the VeilPiercer Parallax architecture.

20. Control Logic

Conceptually:

def vpla_step(ship, emitters, receivers, track_store):

    ship_state = read_ship_state(ship)

    observations = []

    for emitter in emitters:

        measurement = emitter.observe()

        if measurement.valid:

            observations.append({
                "sensor_position": emitter.position,
                "sensor_orientation": emitter.orientation,
                "bearing": measurement.bearing,
                "elevation": measurement.elevation,
                "range": measurement.range,
                "signal_quality": measurement.quality,
                "timestamp": measurement.timestamp,
            })

    correlated = correlate_observations(
        observations,
        track_store
    )

    solution = solve_multibaseline_geometry(
        correlated,
        ship_state
    )

    trajectory = update_track_filter(
        previous=track_store,
        measurement=solution
    )

    confidence = score_confidence(
        geometry=solution.geometry_quality,
        source_count=len(correlated),
        persistence=trajectory.persistence,
        uncertainty=trajectory.uncertainty
    )

    return {
        "position": trajectory.position,
        "velocity": trajectory.velocity,
        "uncertainty": trajectory.uncertainty,
        "confidence": confidence
    }

This remains a conceptual defensive sensing loop.

23. Failure Modes
Poor Baseline Geometry

Emitters are too close together or nearly collinear.

Effect:

weak depth resolution

Mitigation:

use wider physical baseline
combine observations over ship motion
Low Optical Return

One channel receives weak data.

Mitigation:

reduce channel weight
maintain previous track
use other sensors
False Correlation

Different emitters are accidentally assigned to different contacts.

Mitigation:

time alignment
motion consistency
range agreement
multi-frame persistence

The Parallax Engine already treats false correlation and multi-contact ambiguity as a central problem and recommends maintaining competing hypotheses until the track separates.

Platform Motion Error

Ship motion is measured incorrectly.

Effect:

false target motion

Mitigation:

high-quality inertial state
time synchronization
sensor calibration
Optical Obstruction

One or more channels lose line of sight.

Mitigation:

continue track with surviving channels
increase uncertainty
fuse radar/relay observations
24. Realism Boundary

VPLA does not create perfect location data simply because three beams are present.

Precision remains limited by:

baseline length,
pointing accuracy,
timing accuracy,
target range,
target motion,
optical return quality,
ship vibration,
calibration,
atmospheric or environmental effects,
synchronization accuracy.

The correct output is therefore:

Contact estimated here, within this uncertainty volume, with this confidence.

not:

Contact is exactly here.

That matches the existing VeilPiercer philosophy of preserving uncertainty rather than presenting uncertain tracks as absolute coordinates.

26. Final PoC Statement

The VeilPiercer Prism-Lock Array (VPLA) is a defensive multi-angle optical sensing concept designed to improve relative position, range, velocity, and trajectory estimates for spacecraft contacts.

Multiple optical or infrared observations are produced from known locations and orientations. Their geometry is fused with own-ship telemetry and time-series tracking to produce a confidence-scored three-dimensional contact state.

Its core principle is:

$$ \boxed{ \text{Known baselines} + \text{multiple optical bearings} + \text{time continuity} = \text{better constrained contact geometry} } $$

Its purpose is not autonomous engagement.

Its purpose is navigation, deconfliction, docking, collision avoidance, debris monitoring, and persistent defensive awareness.

Working doctrine

One beam sees direction. Two beams discover depth. Three beams make the geometry argue with itself until only the most probable location remains.
